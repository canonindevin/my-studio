    How Franklin used his

    Around 1730 Benjamin Franklin ruled a small book with red ink: seven columns for the days, thirteen rows for his virtues. Each evening he put a black dot against any virtue he had slipped on.

    He gave one virtue his strict attention each week and left the rest to ordinary care. Thirteen weeks made a course, and four courses made a year.

    He never got a clean book. Order defeated him for life. He wrote that he was still "a better and a happier man than I otherwise should have been."
