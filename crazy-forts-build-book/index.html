The one ball trick

Most frustration with this kit comes from balls turned the wrong way. Do these four things on every ball and the roof sticks line up on their own.

  1. Arrow up, every time.The little arrows on each ball point to the top hole. Posts go straight in there.
  2. Flat sticks go in the belt.That's the ring of holes around the ball's middle.
  3. Turn before you push.Spin the ball so its slanted holes aim between your flat sticks, toward the middle of the square. If a roof stick won't line up, pop the ball off, give it an eighth of a turn, and re-seat it.
  4. Push and twist.Each stick goes in with a twist until it stops. Half-seated sticks are why forts fall apart.

House rules for fort day

  • Sort first.Piles of 10 sticks and a bowl of balls. Hailey can run this while you read the step.
  • Build on a rug.Balls roll on hard floors, and carpet grips the bottom row.
  • You hold, they click.Above knee height, you steady the frame and the kids push sticks in.
  • Light sheets only.Flat sheets plus clothespins or chip clips. Heavy blankets make roofs sag.
  • Sit in it, never on it.No hanging on sticks. A tug on one post can bring the whole frame down.
  • Declan stays close.The box says ages 5+, so his jobs are handing, pressing, and inspecting, right next to you.

Pick a fort

Each one fits inside your two kits. Start with the tunnel if this is your first build, and save the Big Top and Base Camp for when you've got the ball trick down.
